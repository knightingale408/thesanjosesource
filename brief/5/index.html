Before the Dec. 1 vote

San José Council weighs City-run plan for $11.7M in federal rental assistance

Staff propose a City-run program for about 120 households; HUD did not approve the 2024 County-run plan. The federal money, awarded in 2021, must be spent by Sept. 30, 2030.

By J Gonzales · Nov. 23, 2026 · Corrected and updated Nov. 25, 2026

Correction November 25, 2026: we corrected the agenda page for the rental assistance item (p. 23, not p. 21) and two lines that described Dec. 1 votes as already decided. We also changed wording to match the City record's own terms ("rental assistance," "exemption"). The original wording is under Corrections and updates at the end of this page.

How to weigh in: The Council meets Tuesday, Dec. 1, 1:30 p.m., at City Hall or by Zoom. Items 8.3 (HOME-ARP rental assistance plan, a public hearing), 8.1 (construction-tax exemption) and 2.15 (payroll system). Email city.clerk@sanjoseca.gov by 5 p.m. Monday, Nov. 30, with the item number in the subject line, or use eComment on the Legistar calendar (agenda, p. 4). The draft HOME-ARP plan is open for public review on the Housing Department's website through Dec. 9 (memo, p. 10).

What matters this week

  • Deciding (Dec. 1): Whether to approve a revised plan for San José's federal HOME-ARP grant and send it to the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) (record, agenda p. 23).
  • The money: $11,693,829 in federal funds, first awarded in 2021. All of it must be spent by Sept. 30, 2030 (memo pp. 1–2).
  • For residents: Staff expect to serve about 120 households with 12 to 18 months of rental assistance and support services, starting with people already in City shelters (pp. 5–6).

Rental assistance: the revised plan

In 2021, San José was awarded $11,676,334 from HOME-ARP, a one-time federal program for people who are homeless or at risk of homelessness. HUD added $17,495 in April 2025 to fix an administrative error, bringing the total to $11,693,829 (memo p. 2).

The Council approved the first plan for the money on March 28, 2023. On Dec. 17, 2024, it approved a change: the County of Santa Clara would run the rental assistance program. HUD did not approve the revised plan, so "the proposed County-administered program could not be implemented" (p. 3).

The sticking point was who could apply. HUD requires the program to be open to four groups, from people who are homeless to people at risk of losing their housing. The earlier plan relied on the County's existing homeless-services waitlist, which staff say does not rank people at risk of homelessness for this kind of help. The County said it could not expand that system to all four groups or keep a separate waitlist for the program, so the City-County agreement "will be terminated" (pp. 2, 4–5).

The memo says "no HOME-ARP funds were disbursed under the County agreement," and that $453,027 in City administrative costs has already been spent (p. 9).

Under the new plan, the City would keep its own waitlist and hire a nonprofit to run the program, with a request for proposals in "winter 2026." First priority would go to households in City shelters, with preference for young adults ages 18 to 24, families with children and people with lower vulnerability scores; everyone else who qualifies would come next, again with preference for young adults and families (pp. 5–6).

How the money would shift (p. 7):

  • Rental assistance: $4,500,000 → $7,317,495
  • Support services: $2,424,884 → $2,624,884
  • Homelessness prevention: $3,000,000 → $0. Staff say the City would support the County's prevention program with $5,430,576 in Measure E money instead, also on the Dec. 1 agenda (p. 3, note 4).
  • Administration: $1,751,450 for the City → $1,251,450, plus $500,000 for the nonprofit

Staff will return to the Council to approve the agreement with the nonprofit (p. 9).

Also on the agenda

A broader construction-tax exemption for affordable housing (8.1)

The City's exemptions from two construction taxes date to 1978 and cover only projects funded under four older federal housing programs. Projects funded by newer sources, such as federal tax credits or state and local grants, don't qualify (memo, p. 2). The proposed ordinance would exempt deed-restricted affordable housing financed by any government housing subsidy. It would not cover affordable units in market-rate buildings (p. 3; record).

The cost: three projects in the pipeline "were estimated to generate approximately $1.2 million in construction tax revenue," which the City expected to collect in fiscal 2028-29 or later. The exemption would apply "indefinitely," and the Housing Department would report on its use within 12 months (p. 4).

A 2015 payroll-system contract, extended to 2031 (2.15)

CherryRoad Technologies runs the PeopleSoft system behind City human resources, payroll, budget and timekeeping. Its contract, first approved in 2015, ends Dec. 31, 2026. Staff ask for five one-year options through Dec. 31, 2031, with support "estimated at $500,000 annually" from the General Fund. The City "does not anticipate initiating" a review of a replacement system until fiscal 2028-29 (memo, pp. 1–3; record). It's a consent item, so it would be approved with other routine items in one vote unless a councilmember pulls it (agenda p. 10).
