Brief #3 · Council, Nov. 17 · Published November 16, 2026

Data center construction helped push a San José wastewater bid 15% over estimate

City staff say limited competition and a Bay Area building surge, including data centers, left only two contractors bidding on an $11,248,000 contract.

By J Gonzales

By J Gonzales · Nov. 16, 2026

What matters this week

  • Deciding: Whether to hire Blocka Construction to replace heating, ventilation and air conditioning in eight Regional Wastewater Facility buildings, and Black & Veatch to advise the City on a pump-station overhaul through 2036.
  • Cost: $11,248,000 plus a $1,687,200 contingency for the heating contract and up to $16,885,000 for Black & Veatch, both from the plant's capital fund (Fund 512).
  • For residents: New systems for equipment the City says is 30 to 60 years old, with construction from January 2027 to about October 2028. Pump-station construction is not expected to start until late 2031.

Only two contractors bid, and staff point to data center construction

San José's City Council will decide Tuesday, Nov. 17, whether to award Blocka Construction $11,248,000 to replace heating, ventilation and air conditioning in eight support buildings at the San José-Santa Clara Regional Wastewater Facility. The staff memo says the equipment is 30 to 60 years old and "no longer meets current operational or energy efficiency requirements" (p. 1).

Only two contractors bid. Blocka's price was 15% above the engineer's estimate of $9,800,000; the other bid was $11,845,000 (p. 3). Staff call the bid reasonable and point to limited competition and a local construction surge, "including continued data center development in the Bay Area" (p. 3).

This is the second try. The City cancelled the first bid in May after "font display issues" in the plans (p. 2). With a 15% contingency of $1,687,200 and design and management costs, the whole project is budgeted at $18,824,284 (p. 5). Construction would begin in January 2027, with substantial completion expected in October 2028 (p. 2).

A $16,885,000 adviser contract would start years before pump-station construction

The second item would hire Black & Veatch as the City's "owner's advisor" on the first phase of pump-station repairs, through Dec. 31, 2036. Its fee is about 9.5% of the project's $176,500,000 construction estimate, which the memo calls "a high-level planning estimate" (pp. 3, 6).

The plant's Treatment Plant Advisory Committee recommended both items 8-0, with one member absent, on Nov. 12 (heating letter, adviser letter).

What the pump-station work will finally cost is not known; staff say the estimate "will be further refined" (p. 3). This vote would hire the adviser only. The Council would vote again on a design-build contract, expected in late 2028, and on a guaranteed maximum price, expected in spring 2031, before construction starts (p. 7).

Also on the agenda

The Mayor and four councilmembers propose pausing data center approvals

Mayor Matt Mahan and Councilmembers David Cohen (District 4), Domingo Candelas (District 8), Peter Ortiz (District 5) and Pamela Campos (District 2) ask the Council to pause land use approvals of data centers and large energy use projects until it adopts uniform standards, and to have staff bring an urgency ordinance on Dec. 8, 2026 (item 8.1, added late). Their memo says the pause would apply to approvals "by administrative official, commission, or City Council," so no project gets approved before the standards come to a vote (p. 2). No cost estimate has been posted. Adding the item to Tuesday's agenda takes a two-thirds vote of members present (agenda p. 5).

A $180,000 settlement of a police use-of-force lawsuit

The Council will consider settling Lopez et al. v. City of San José for $180,000 (consent item 2.7): $175,000 for Angelique Lopez and $5,000 for Alberto Ramirez, with each side paying its own legal fees. The City Attorney's memo says the case involves officers' uses of force at a May 5, 2024, fight in a gas station lot near Monterey Road and Southside Drive. The City and officers denied liability. The money would come from the City's claims reserve (pp. 1–2).

Since our last brief

Draft minutes for the Nov. 10 meeting were not posted as of Saturday, Nov. 14, so most votes are not stated here. The Council took up $3 million in loans tied to First Community Housing, a local emergency proclamation that staff said would give the City "immediate access" to its Emergency Reserve Fund, with $5.5 million available, and Apple's request to take over 131,470 square feet of public street.

One result is on the record: the change to Apple's development agreement was passed for publication Nov. 10 and is up for final adoption Nov. 17 as Ordinance No. 31369 (consent item 2.2, agenda p. 6).

Earlier results below are from draft minutes, not yet formally approved.

  • Oct. 20: The Council accepted the City's 2025-26 year-end budget report and adopted its budget changes, 10-0 with one absent. They include $700,000 more for the Biblioteca Latinoamericana renovation.
  • Oct. 27: The Council approved three-year contracts with the Municipal Employees' Federation and two IFPTE Local 21 units, 11-0; a third unit did not ratify. The two IFPTE deals cost about $4.8 million a year, $1.3 million from the General Fund.

J Gonzales's take

Replacing aging wastewater equipment is necessary; accepting higher prices without scrutiny is not. Staff cite construction demand, including data centers, but that does not establish that pausing approvals would lower this project's costs. For voters, the stakes are reliable sewer service and disciplined use of public money. The adviser agreement deserves scrutiny too: a spending ceiling is not a measure of performance. Will Council require public reporting that compares promised costs and deadlines with actual delivery—and explains every significant change?
