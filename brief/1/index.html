Council approves up to $6.9M fee waiver and $5.7M housing loan; police recruiting overtime runs $631,986 over budget

Also: what the Council decided on housing and homelessness from Sept. 15 to Oct. 6, and the police auditor’s year-end report coming Oct. 20.

By J Gonzales

Corrected October 14, 2026 · Updated October 17, 2026

We relied only on empty fields in the city’s data feed and did not check the City Clerk’s draft minutes, which were posted after each meeting and before this brief went out. Those minutes record what happened to every item, and two of the actions carry dollar figures. We have corrected the affected lines and added the outcomes below.

This brief was first published October 12, 2026. On October 13, 2026 we added a section on police recruiting overtime and corrected two summary lines that the new section made inaccurate. What matters this week

  • Two Council decisions came with dollar figures. On Sept. 15 the Council approved a tax and fee waiver of up to $6,934,618 for Phase 1 of a housing development at 3896 Stevens Creek Blvd. On Oct. 6 it approved a loan commitment of up to $5,700,000 for affordable housing at 1510 North 1st Street. Sources: Sept 15 draft minutes, p. 11, Oct 6 draft minutes, p. 9
  • Police recruiting overtime ran $631,986 over budget. The Police Department’s recruiting unit spent $1,052,615 on overtime in fiscal year 2025-26 against a $420,629 overtime budget, according to its annual report. Source: SJPD report, p. 9
  • Each Council item we reviewed from Sept. 15 to Oct. 6 was approved, adopted or accepted. That includes the homelessness work plan, the Measure T bond status report and the Arena Hotel and Homekey plan. The one exception: the Arena Hotel item was dropped from the Sept. 22 agenda, then adopted Oct. 6. Sources: Sept 15 draft minutes, p. 9, Sept 22 draft minutes, p. 10, Sept 29 draft minutes, p. 7, Oct 6 draft minutes, p. 10
  • Next: Oct. 20. The full Council hears the Independent Police Auditor’s 2025 year-end report and the Police Department’s response to its 12 recommendations. Source: file 26-1078

The money

Housing: a fee waiver and a loan

  • 3896 Stevens Creek Blvd. (Sept. 15): after a public hearing, the Council adopted Resolution RES2026-290, approving a tax and fee waiver of up to $6,934,618 for Phase 1 of the housing development. Vote: 10-0, one member absent. Source: Sept 15 draft minutes, p. 11
  • 1510 North 1st Street (Oct. 6): the Council adopted Resolution RES2026-318, a construction-permanent loan commitment of up to $5,700,000 to the Santa Clara County Housing Authority or an affiliate, from the Low and Moderate Income Housing Asset Fund. It also adopted Ordinance No. 31355, moving the same amount from the Housing Project Reserve to Housing Loans and Grants. Vote: 10-0, one member absent. Source: Oct 6 draft minutes, p. 9

For the other actions below, the draft minutes do not list a dollar amount. The staff reports attached to each item may; we have not yet checked all of them.

Police recruiting overtime

Added October 13, 2026. This was on a Council committee’s October 15 agenda.

On Thursday, October 15, at 1:30 p.m., the City Council’s Public Safety, Finance & Strategic Support Committee was scheduled to hear the Police Department’s annual recruitment and hiring report for fiscal year 2025-26 (agenda item (d)2, CC 26-111). Staff recommended that the committee accept the report. It is an informational report, not a vote to spend money. Source: October 15 committee agenda

Recruiting Unit, FY 2025-26BudgetSpentSource
Overtime$420,629$1,052,615Report, p. 9
Personnel (spending as of June 29, 2026)$2,612,041$3,592,548Report, p. 9
Equipment$365,040Not statedReport, p. 9
Total allocation$2,977,081Not statedReport, p. 9

The report states that overtime spending exceeded the overtime budget by $631,986.

The report does not say whether overtime is counted inside the personnel figures, so the rows should not be added together.

Why, according to the department

The report says overtime is high mainly because of the recruiting events staff attend. Most of those events are on weekends or weekday evenings, and two or three often run at the same time in different places, which takes extra staff. The report says overtime will be monitored and reviewed for possible adjustments in future budgets. It does not propose a specific change. Source: Report, p. 9

Context from the same report

  • As of June 26, 2026, the department had 1,167 budgeted sworn positions: 1,061 filled and 106 vacant. It counted 897 officers as “street ready.” Source: Report, p. 2
  • It hired 116 recruits and lateral officers in FY 2025-26, compared with 115 the year before. Applications rose to 3,135 from 1,198. Source: Report, p. 3
  • The department projected 81 sworn departures for the year; 119 officers retired, resigned, were terminated or resigned in lieu of termination, 38 more than projected. Source: Report, p. 9

Note on one figure: the report gives two departure counts for the same year: 119 on page 9 and 120 on page 11. We use 119, the figure the report compares with its projection of 81. We have not been told which is correct.

What happens next

  • Oct. 20, City Council (item 4.1): the Interim Independent Police Auditor presents the 2025 year-end report, covering complaints and audits of Internal Affairs investigations in 2025. The Police Department’s memo responds to the report’s 12 policy recommendations and says it agrees or partly agrees with most of them. Staff recommend that the Council accept both the report and the department’s response. Neither memo lists a dollar amount, and the agenda describes the item as one that involves no approval of a City action. Sources: file 26-1078, auditor’s memo, Police Department response
  • How to comment on Oct. 20: the agenda says written comments can be sent through eComment on the Legistar calendar, or emailed to city.clerk@sanjoseca.gov no earlier than 10 days before the meeting and no later than 5:00 p.m. the day before. You can also speak in person (Speaker’s Card) or by phone at (888) 475 4499, Webinar ID 982 2147 4336. Sources: October 20 agenda, meeting page
