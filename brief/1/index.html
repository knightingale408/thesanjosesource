This week’s brief

  • The City Clerk’s draft minutes record what the Council did on every item in our original pull from September 15 through October 6. Each was approved, adopted or accepted, except the September 22 Arena Hotel item, which was dropped from that agenda and then adopted October 6. Source: Sept 15 draft minutes, p. 9, Sept 22 draft minutes, p. 10, Sept 29 draft minutes, p. 7, Oct 6 draft minutes, p. 10
  • Two of those actions carry dollar figures in the minutes: a tax and fee waiver of up to $6,934,618 for Phase 1 of a housing development at 3896 Stevens Creek Blvd. (Sept. 15), and a loan commitment of up to $5,700,000 for affordable housing at 1510 North 1st Street (Oct. 6). Source: Sept 15 draft minutes, p. 11, Oct 6 draft minutes, p. 9. The police recruiting item below also includes dollar figures.
  • Added October 13, 2026: San José police recruiting overtime was $1,052,615 in fiscal year 2025-26, against a $420,629 budget. The department’s annual report says that exceeded the budget by $631,986. A Council committee hears the report Thursday, October 15. Source: SJPD report, p. 9
  • Two items are still ahead: the police recruiting report at the Public Safety, Finance & Strategic Support Committee on October 15, and the 2025 Independent Police Auditor year-end report at the full Council on October 20. Sources: October 15 committee agenda, 26-1078
  • Arena Hotel and Homekey: on October 6 the Council adopted Resolution RES2026-319 (10-0-1), authorizing exclusive negotiations with Episcopal Community Services and making the City the sole grantee under the state Homekey agreement. Source: Oct 6 draft minutes, p. 10, 26-1035
  • The Measure T status report was renumbered from 26-1002 to 26-1045 and accepted on September 29 (9-0-2). Source: Sept 29 draft minutes, p. 7, 26-1045

Added: Police recruiting overtime (Council committee, October 15)

Added October 13, 2026. This is a Council committee item, so it was outside the original pull of full City Council agendas.

On Thursday, October 15, at 1:30 p.m., the City Council’s Public Safety, Finance & Strategic Support Committee will hear the Police Department’s annual recruitment and hiring report for fiscal year 2025-26 (agenda item (d)2, CC 26-111). Staff recommend that the committee accept the report. It is an informational report, not a vote to spend money. Source: October 15 committee agenda

The report states that overtime spending exceeded the overtime budget by $631,986.

The report does not say whether overtime is counted inside the personnel figures, so the rows should not be added together.

Why, according to the department

The department attributes the high overtime to the recruiting events staff attend. Most of those events are on weekends or weekday evenings, and two or three often run at the same time in different places, which takes extra staff. The report says overtime will be monitored and reviewed for possible adjustments in future budgets. It does not propose a specific change. Source: Report, p. 9

Context from the same report

  • As of June 26, 2026, the department had 1,167 budgeted sworn positions: 1,061 filled and 106 vacant. It counted 897 officers as “street ready.” Source: Report, p. 2
  • It hired 116 recruits and lateral officers in FY 2025-26, compared with 115 the year before. Applications rose to 3,135 from 1,198. We have not yet confirmed how the report counts applications and hires (for example, unique applicants or total submissions) or whether both years use the same counting period. Source: Report, p. 3
  • The department projected 81 sworn departures for the year; 119 officers retired, resigned, were terminated or resigned in lieu of termination, 38 more than projected. Source: Report, p. 9

Note on one figure: the report gives two departure counts for the same year: 119 on page 9 and 120 on page 11. We use 119, the figure the report compares with its projection of 81. We have not been told which is correct.
