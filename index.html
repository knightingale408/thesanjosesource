Long agendas. Short brief.

City Council agendas, staff memos, and budget reports are public — and long, dense, and scattered. The San Jose Source is the short version: what is on the agenda, what it costs, and where it sits in the budget.

Who writes it, how we use AI, and how it’s funded →

What’s in each brief

Fewer matters, covered more completely. Facts first. Opinion labeled.

  1. What’s decided

    What the City proposed or decided on public money, housing, fees and public safety, from the official record.

  2. The money

    What the financial terms actually establish, and who is affected.

  3. What’s next

    The next vote or deadline, and how to comment while there’s still time.

  4. J Gonzales’s take

    A short, clearly marked opinion section. Kept separate from the facts.

When it lands

Weekly, before Council meets — so you can read the money items while there’s still time to show up or write in.

The sourcing promise

Every factual claim links to the City’s official record, such as agendas and staff reports on Legistar and City budget documents. No party line. No guesses about motives. No rumors. If something is wrong, the correction goes at the top.

Promised vs. Actual

What the City said a project would cost and when it would finish, compared with what actually happened, using only dates and dollars the record states.

Example: a June 2021 City status report expected Fire Station 8 to be finished in mid-2024. Its crew began operating there on July 22, 2026.
