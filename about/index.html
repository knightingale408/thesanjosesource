San José decisions, public money, and what happens next

By J Gonzales · Updated October 18, 2026

The San Jose Source is a free weekly brief on San José City Council money decisions: what is on the agenda, what it costs, where it sits in the budget, and what happened after the vote.

Who writes it

The San Jose Source is written and published by J Gonzales. It is independent: it is not run by, paid by or affiliated with the City of San José, any elected official, campaign or political party.

How we report

  • Every factual claim links to the City’s official record, mainly agendas and staff reports on Legistar and City budget documents.
  • Facts come first. Opinion is kept in a separate, clearly labeled “My take” section.
  • No party line, no guesses about motives, no rumors.
  • In our Promised vs. Actual tracker, “promised” means the City’s own published targets, not contractual commitments. We calculate a delay or a budget difference only when City documents give exact dates or amounts.

How we use AI

We use AI tools, including Claude and Grok, to find City documents, draft summaries, do arithmetic and check claims against their sources. AI does not decide what we publish. A person reads the linked source behind every claim, and every page is approved by J Gonzales before it goes live.

How it’s funded

The brief is free, with no paywall, no ads and no paid subscriptions. Readers who want to help cover research and publishing tools can make a one-time $25 contribution. It is not tax-deductible, and it does not buy coverage or influence what we report. If that ever changes, for example a sponsor, we will say so here and label it wherever it appears.

Corrections, updates and clarifications

When we change a published page, we say so on that page, with the date, and list the original wording. We use three labels:

  • Correction: something we published was wrong. The correction goes at the top of the page.
  • Update: new information was added, such as a vote result, or a headline or presentation was changed.
  • Clarification: the facts were right, but the wording could mislead, so we made it clearer.
