The heating memo says limited competition and a local construction surge, including data center development, "may have" contributed to higher bids; it does not say the surge left only two bidders. ## What matters this week

- **Deciding:** Whether to hire Blocka Construction to replace heating, ventilation and air conditioning in eight Regional Wastewater Facility buildings, and Black & Veatch to advise the City on a pump-station overhaul through 2036.
- **Cost:** [$11,248,000 plus a $1,687,200 contingency](https://sanjose.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=8307264&GUID=AA6B974E-292A-44B8-80B8-210432F2460B) for the heating contract and [up to $16,885,000](https://sanjose.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=8307265&GUID=10E9141B-21D6-4432-99CA-4FAC4A1EF046) for Black & Veatch, both from the plant's capital fund. With design and management, the heating project's full budget is $18,824,284.
- **For residents:** New systems for equipment the City says is 30 to 60 years old, with construction from January 2027 to about October 2028. Pump-station construction is not expected to start until late 2031. The plant treats wastewater from eight South Bay cities and four special districts ([adviser memo](https://legistar.granicus.com/sanjose/attachments/2f73d643-fdb8-4cbe-9b58-038f7164f23d.pdf), p. 2), and staff say the adviser contract has no additional impact on the City's General Fund (p. 8).

## Only two contractors bid, and staff point to data center construction

San José's City Council will decide Tuesday, Nov. 17, whether to award Blocka Construction $11,248,000 to replace heating, ventilation and air conditioning in eight support buildings at the San José-Santa Clara Regional Wastewater Facility. The [staff memo](https://legistar.granicus.com/sanjose/attachments/5d0c4f99-3de4-4886-b417-aef93334c8f9.pdf) says the equipment is 30 to 60 years old and "no longer meets current operational or energy efficiency requirements" (p. 1).

Only two contractors bid. Blocka's price was $1,448,000, or 15%, above the engineer's estimate of $9,800,000; the other bid was $11,845,000 (p. 3). Staff call the bid reasonable and point to limited competition and a local construction surge, "including continued data center development in the Bay Area" (p. 3).

This is the second try. The City canceled the first bid in May after "font display issues" in the plans (p. 2). Counting a 15% contingency of $1,687,200 and design and management costs, the whole project is budgeted at $18,824,284 (p. 5). Construction would begin in January 2027, with substantial completion expected in October 2028 (p. 2).

## A $16,885,000 adviser contract would start years before pump-station construction

The second item would hire Black & Veatch as the City's "owner's advisor" on the first phase of pump-station repairs, through Dec. 31, 2036. Its fee is about 9.5% of the project's $176,500,000 construction estimate, which the [memo](https://legistar.granicus.com/sanjose/attachments/2f73d643-fdb8-4cbe-9b58-038f7164f23d.pdf) calls "a high-level planning estimate" (pp. 3, 6).

The plant's Treatment Plant Advisory Committee recommended both items 8-0, with one member absent, on Nov. 12 ([heating letter](https://legistar.granicus.com/sanjose/attachments/8d34909f-620b-44ed-942f-67596ded3434.pdf), [adviser letter](https://legistar.granicus.com/sanjose/attachments/4115608d-8635-4ad6-a18f-48a85a742260.pdf)).

What the pump-station work will finally cost is not known; staff say the estimate "will be further refined" (p. 3). This vote would hire the adviser only. The Council would vote twice more before construction starts (p. 7). The schedule:

- **Late 2028:** a design-build contract
- **Spring 2031:** a guaranteed maximum price
- **Late 2031:** construction expected to start
- **Dec. 31, 2036:** the adviser agreement ends

## Also on the agenda

### The Mayor and four councilmembers propose pausing data center approvals

Mayor Matt Mahan and Councilmembers David Cohen (District 4), Domingo Candelas (District 8), Peter Ortiz (District 5) and Pamela Campos (District 2) ask the Council to [pause land use approvals of data centers and large energy use projects](https://sanjose.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=8319338&GUID=36D6A965-5868-43B5-913F-38A06F3E770E) until it adopts uniform standards, and to have staff bring an urgency ordinance on Dec. 8, 2026 (added to the agenda late). Their [memo](https://legistar.granicus.com/sanjose/attachments/606a2feb-7963-48e4-af7d-ded74f42e041.pdf) says the pause would apply to approvals "by administrative official, commission, or City Council," so no project gets approved before the standards come to a vote (p. 2). It is the second item on Tuesday's agenda that touches data centers: staff say construction activity, including data center development, may have contributed to higher bids on the heating contract. No cost estimate has been posted. Adding the item to Tuesday's agenda takes a two-thirds vote of members present (agenda p. 5).

### A $180,000 settlement of a police use-of-force lawsuit

The Council will consider [settling Lopez et al. v. City of San José for $180,000](https://sanjose.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=8308950&GUID=67412A9A-30B0-43C1-94AF-8CEA4E455AAE) (a consent item, approved with other routine items in a single vote unless a councilmember pulls it): $175,000 for Angelique Lopez and $5,000 for Alberto Ramirez, with each side paying its own legal fees. The [City Attorney's memo](https://legistar.granicus.com/sanjose/attachments/2657cd2f-7c7f-4d6a-ac14-100be03cc838.pdf) says the case involves officers' uses of force at a May 5, 2024, fight in a gas station lot near Monterey Road and Southside Drive. The City and officers denied liability. The money would come from the City's claims reserve (pp. 1–2).

## J Gonzales's take

Replacing wastewater equipment that is 30 to 60 years old is necessary, and the staff memo explains why the heating bid came in high. The bigger long-term commitment is the adviser agreement: up to $16,885,000 through 2036, for a project whose cost staff still call "a high-level planning estimate." A spending ceiling is not a measure of performance. Before approving it, the Council should require regular public reports that compare the adviser's promised costs and deadlines with what is actually delivered, and explain every significant change.
